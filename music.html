Digital Accessibility

Accessible Music Instruction

Make notation, listening, performance, composition, and music technology accessible.

Module Overview

Make notation, listening, performance, composition, and music technology accessible.

Digital accessibility helps students engage with the discipline through multiple usable formats and methods. Plan access to source materials, software, activities, and assessment from the start, while preserving the essential learning goals.

Learning Objectives

  • Identify barriers in scores, recordings, instruments, and music software.
  • Provide accessible notation and meaningful musical descriptions.
  • Make listening and performance activities inclusive without changing learning goals.
  • Offer accessible ways to compose, rehearse, and demonstrate learning.

How to Make Music Materials More Accessible

Music Notation and Scores

A score image alone may be inaccessible to learners using screen readers or magnification.

  • Provide structured, machine-readable notation where feasible, such as MusicXML, and confirm compatibility with the learner’s tools.
  • Offer enlarged scores, braille music, or verbal descriptions as appropriate.
  • Describe key, meter, instrumentation, tempo, form, and significant passages.
  • Check reading order and navigation through parts and measures.
Listening Examples and Audio

Listening activities need clear instructions and alternatives for students who cannot hear all audio information.

  • Provide track identification, timing references, and descriptions of musical structure.
  • Use transcripts for spoken introductions, interviews, and lectures.
  • Discuss equivalent ways to demonstrate analytical understanding with the learner and accessibility staff.
Captions, Lyrics, and Transcripts

Music videos and lectures often combine speech, lyrics, performance, and visual instruction.

  • Caption spoken words and meaningful non-speech audio where appropriate.
  • Provide lyrics or text when rights and course permissions allow.
  • Describe important visual performance cues, gestures, or instrument techniques.
Rhythm, Pitch, and Musical Structure

Musical ideas can be represented through multiple complementary formats.

  • Pair notation with structured text, audio demonstrations, or tactile materials where useful.
  • Explain patterns, intervals, repetitions, dynamics, and changes explicitly.
  • Avoid using color as the sole indicator of notes or sections.
Instruments, Rehearsals, and Performance

Accessibility may require adapting physical interactions, rehearsal communication, or performance environments.

  • Share rehearsal materials and cues in advance.
  • Describe demonstrations and avoid relying solely on pointing or gesture.
  • Consider adaptive equipment, seating, lighting, and accessible routes.
Composition and Digital Audio Workstations

DAW and notation software vary in keyboard and screen-reader support.

  • Test track navigation, controls, plug-ins, and export workflows with the actual software version.
  • Provide keyboard commands and descriptive labels when available.
  • Allow alternate composition workflows that meet the same learning objectives.
Music Analysis and Spectrograms

Waveforms, spectrograms, and annotated scores encode meaning visually.

  • Describe axes, time, frequency, amplitude, and significant changes.
  • Offer accessible numeric data or verbal analysis of important features.
  • Use labels, patterns, and text rather than color alone.
Accessible Assignments and Feedback

Make assessment requirements clear and provide equivalent ways to submit work.

  • Use accessible rubrics and instructions.
  • Provide text feedback alongside audio-only feedback when needed.
  • Check that recordings, scores, and presentation files are usable in their final format.

Key Takeaways

  • Music accessibility includes scores, sound, physical performance, and software workflows.
  • Use structured notation and meaningful descriptions alongside audio and visual material.
  • Maintain musical learning outcomes while offering accessible ways to participate.
